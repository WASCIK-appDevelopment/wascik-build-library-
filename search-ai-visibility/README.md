# Search and AI Visibility

Reusable patterns extracted from the WASCIK Growth Engine for making a service-business website understandable, crawlable, citable, locally discoverable, and measurable.

## Use this module for

- technical search-discovery foundations
- AI-search and answer-engine visibility
- local service-business identity consistency
- keyword and opportunity mapping
- original content planning
- authority and review development
- conversion measurement
- controlled publishing and verification

This module complements `growth-engine-marketing/`. That module focuses on funnels, attribution, and advertising workspaces; this module focuses on discovery, authority, citations, and continuing search improvement.

## Core operating model

A useful growth engine has six connected parts:

1. Technical discovery foundation.
2. Business and search opportunity map.
3. Continuing original-content engine.
4. Authority and local-presence development.
5. Conversion improvement.
6. Measurement and revision loop.

Do not treat publishing, crawling, indexing, ranking, citation, traffic, leads, and sales as interchangeable states.

## Technical discovery checklist

Confirm each item in source and again on the deployed site:

- one canonical production origin
- indexable pages return successful responses
- self-referencing canonical URLs
- unique page titles and descriptions
- XML sitemap containing only intended public pages
- robots rules that permit intended search and AI crawlers
- structured data that matches visible page content
- mobile usability and accessible navigation
- no broken internal routes
- no accidental `noindex` on sales or resource pages
- intentional `noindex` on genuine completion-only pages
- search-engine ownership verification files remain intact

Never copy a production verification token into a reusable module. Verification files are project-specific controlled assets.

## Structured-data pattern

Use the smallest truthful graph supported by visible content. Typical service-business types include:

- `WebSite`
- `Organization`, `ProfessionalService`, or the most accurate business subtype
- `Person` for a named founder or author
- `Service`
- `Offer` only when price and terms are actually visible
- `FAQPage` only for visible questions and answers
- `BreadcrumbList` for real navigation hierarchy
- `Article` for substantive authored resources

Do not invent ratings, reviews, locations, opening hours, prices, qualifications, or service claims for schema.

## AI-search discovery

AI systems may find a business through normal web search, direct crawling, cited third-party profiles, and structured page content. Strengthen those paths through:

- clear brand and service descriptions
- consistent entity names across owned and third-party profiles
- explicit service area and delivery method
- substantive question-and-answer content
- named authorship and meaningful update dates
- crawlable text rather than image-only claims
- internal links connecting services, proof, resources, and contact actions
- reputable third-party citations and genuine reviews
- an optional `llms.txt` orientation file

An `llms.txt` file is an orientation aid, not a ranking guarantee or substitute for crawlable pages, authority, and customer evidence.

## Local service-area safeguards

When customers are not served at a public storefront:

- describe the business as a service-area business
- state that work is remote or performed at the customer's location by appointment
- keep the private address hidden on public profiles where platform policy allows
- never invite walk-in visits
- keep the legal or verification address private inside platform accounts when required
- use only cities, ZIP codes, or regions actually served
- keep name, telephone, website, category, hours, and service area consistent

## Measurement loop

Review on a stable weekly cadence:

- indexed pages and exclusions
- impressions, clicks, and average position
- branded versus non-branded queries
- local-profile calls, messages, and website visits
- form submissions, telephone clicks, and email clicks
- landing-page conversion rate
- third-party citations and genuine reviews
- AI-answer mentions and linked citations
- pages gaining or losing visibility
- the next evidence-based test

Record the source, date, location/personalization conditions, query, observed result, and follow-up action. A manual search result is a snapshot, not a permanent ranking claim.

## Publishing controls

1. Inspect the authoritative branch and deployed production separately.
2. Make narrowly scoped changes.
3. Build and validate.
4. Commit only within the owner's authorized task.
5. Publish production only under the project's explicit deployment rule.
6. Verify the live response after publication.
7. Request recrawl or indexing only after a meaningful live change.
8. Record the resulting state without overstating it.
